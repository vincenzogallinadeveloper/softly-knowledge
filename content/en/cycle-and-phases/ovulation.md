## Glance
Ovulation is when an ovary releases an egg — the most fertile point of your cycle.

## Overview
Ovulation is the moment an ovary releases a mature egg. It's the turning point of the cycle: the
first half builds up to it, and the second half follows it.

In a cycle of about 28 days, ovulation often happens around day 14 — but the timing varies. A
helpful way to think about it is that ovulation usually occurs somewhere from 10 to 16 days before
your next period starts. It can be hard to pinpoint the exact day.

Once released, an egg can be fertilised for only about 12 to 24 hours. Sperm, though, can survive
in the body for several days, so the "fertile window" is wider than a single day — roughly the few
days leading up to ovulation and the day itself.

## Deep
The release of the egg is triggered by a short, sharp rise in hormones around mid-cycle, following
the estrogen build-up of the follicular phase. Because sperm can live for a number of days while
the egg lives less than a day, pregnancy is most likely from sex in the days just before
ovulation. Ovulation can shift from cycle to cycle with things like stress or illness, which is
why predicting it exactly is difficult and why tracking several cycles is more reliable than any
single calculation.
