## Glance
You can stay active all through your cycle — and regular movement may ease cramps and PMS.

## Overview
You can be physically active at any point in your cycle. Some people find they have more energy and
can push a little harder at certain times of the month, and less at others — both are normal, and
neither is a reason to stop moving.

Regular activity can actually help with some period-related symptoms. People who exercise regularly
often have fewer painful cramps, and staying active may ease premenstrual symptoms (PMS) too. On
lower-energy days, gentler movement still counts — it doesn't have to be intense to be worthwhile.

There's no need to schedule your life around your cycle, and no single "right" way to move. Noticing
your own energy pattern across the month can simply help you plan in a way that feels good.

## When to see a doctor
- Very heavy training, or exercising so much that your body runs low on energy, can make periods
  become irregular or stop altogether. A regular cycle is generally a sign that things are ticking
  along well, so it's worth talking to a GP if your periods become irregular or stop — this can be
  connected to bone and hormone health over time.
