## Glance
Stalking is repeated, unwanted attention that makes you feel afraid or unsafe — and it is taken seriously.

## Overview
Stalking is a pattern of unwanted, repeated behaviour by someone that leaves you feeling afraid,
watched, or unsafe. It can include being followed, someone turning up where you are, constant calls
or messages, unwanted gifts, monitoring you online or through your phone, or threats. It is often
done by an ex-partner or someone you know, though not always.

The fear it causes is valid, even when each single act might seem "small" to others — it's the
pattern, and how it makes you feel, that matter. Stalking is recognised as a form of abuse, and in
many countries it is a crime. None of it is your fault, and you don't have to just live with it.

If it's safe to do so, keeping a record can help later: dates and times, screenshots of messages,
and what happened. You don't have to work out on your own whether it "counts" — support services can
help you make sense of it and think about your options.

## When to see a doctor
- Contact the **police** to report stalking, and an anti-violence or anti-stalking helpline for
  support and advice (in Italy, **1522** is for violence **and** stalking).
- Keeping evidence and making a safety plan, with help from a specialist service, can both make a
  real difference.

## Red flags
- If you feel in **immediate danger**, contact emergency services now: **112** in the EU (including
  Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
- In Italy, **1522** — free, 24 hours a day, anonymous, multilingual — can help by phone or chat.
