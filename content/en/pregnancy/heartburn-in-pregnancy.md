## Glance
Heartburn is very common in pregnancy — usually harmless, and often eased by small everyday changes.

## Overview
Heartburn and indigestion — a burning feeling or discomfort in the chest, often after eating — are
very common in pregnancy. It's down to pregnancy hormones softening the muscle at the top of the
stomach, and, later on, the growing baby pressing upwards. It's uncomfortable, but not harmful.

Small changes often help: eating smaller meals more often rather than large ones, sitting up while
you eat and for a while afterwards, easing off spicy, fatty or caffeinated things, and not eating
late before bed. Some people find sleeping propped up a little more comfortable.

If that's not enough, a **pharmacist** can suggest antacid remedies that are safe to use in
pregnancy — a good first port of call before it becomes a bigger bother.

## When to see a doctor
- See your **GP or midwife** if simple changes and pharmacy remedies aren't helping, or if you have
  **trouble swallowing**, keep bringing food back up, lose weight without meaning to, or have tummy
  pain — these are worth checking rather than putting down to ordinary heartburn.
