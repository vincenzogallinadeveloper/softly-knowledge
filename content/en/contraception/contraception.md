## Glance
Contraception is the different ways to prevent pregnancy — and there are many to choose from.

## Overview
Contraception — sometimes called birth control — is the range of methods used to prevent pregnancy.
There are many of them, and they work in different ways: some use hormones, some are barriers, some
sit inside the womb, and some are based on tracking the cycle.

How well a method works depends a lot on how it's used. Some methods — like the implant and the
coils — are "fit and forget," and are over 99% effective. Others, like the pill or condoms, are
very effective when used perfectly, but a bit less so with everyday, typical use, because it's
easier to forget a pill or use a condom incorrectly.

One thing worth knowing: **only condoms also protect against sexually transmitted infections
(STIs)**. The other methods prevent pregnancy but not infection, which is why condoms are sometimes
used alongside another method.

There's no single "best" method — the right one depends on your health, your body, and what fits
your life. A GP or a sexual health clinic can talk you through the options and help you choose.

## Deep
Because methods differ so much, it helps to think about what matters to you: how effective it is,
whether it involves remembering something daily, how long it lasts, whether you want hormones or
not, and whether STI protection matters for your situation. Many people change methods over their
life as their needs change. This knowledge base describes how the common methods work so the
choices feel clearer — but the decision, and any prescription or fitting, is one to make with a
clinician.
