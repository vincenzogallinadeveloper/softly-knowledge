## Glance
Stillbirth is the loss of a baby after 24 weeks — uncommon, never your fault, and always met with support.

## Overview
A stillbirth is when a baby is born having died after 24 weeks of pregnancy. (A loss before 24 weeks
is called a miscarriage.) It's uncommon — around 1 in every 250 births — but for the families it
touches, it is a profound loss, and everything about the care that follows is meant to hold you
gently through it.

Sometimes a cause is found — for example a problem with the placenta or the baby's development, or a
health condition — and sometimes, despite tests, **no cause is found at all**. Whatever the reason, a
stillbirth is not something a parent has caused or could have known to prevent.

One thing genuinely does help lower the risk, and it's simple: **telling your midwife straight away
if your baby's movements slow down or change.** Alongside that, the everyday things — going to
antenatal appointments, not smoking, avoiding alcohol, and sleeping on your side from 28 weeks — all
play a part.

## When to see a doctor
- Contact your **midwife or maternity unit immediately** — any time, day or night — if your **baby is
  moving less than usual or their movements feel different.** Never wait to see if it passes; getting
  checked is exactly the right thing to do, and it's never a bother.
- After a loss, specialist midwives and bereavement teams are there for you — for the decisions,
  the practical steps, and the grief. You do not have to face any of it alone, and support groups
  for baby loss are available for as long as you need them.
