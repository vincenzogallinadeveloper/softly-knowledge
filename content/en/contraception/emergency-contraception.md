## Glance
Emergency contraception can prevent pregnancy after unprotected sex — and the sooner, the better.

## Overview
Emergency contraception is a way to prevent pregnancy after sex without contraception, or when your
usual method may have failed (a missed pill, a split condom). There are two kinds, and timing
matters for both.

The first is the **emergency pill**. There are two types: one (levonorgestrel) can be taken up to
3 days — 72 hours — after sex, and another (ulipristal acetate, brand name ellaOne) up to 5 days —
120 hours. The second option is the **copper coil (IUD)**, which a clinician can fit up to 5 days
after sex; it's the most effective form of emergency contraception, and it can then stay in as
ongoing contraception if you'd like.

For all of these, **the sooner you use them, the better they tend to work** — so it's worth acting
quickly rather than waiting. You can get emergency contraception from a pharmacy, a GP, a sexual
health clinic, or a walk-in centre, often free.

## When to see a doctor
- Because it's time-sensitive, don't wait — a pharmacist can provide the emergency pill quickly, and
  a sexual health clinic or GP can fit the IUD.
- Emergency contraception is about preventing pregnancy, not infection. If there's any chance of a
  sexually transmitted infection, a sexual health clinic can advise on testing.
- If your period is then more than a few days late, or very different from usual, it's worth taking
  a pregnancy test and speaking to a clinician.
