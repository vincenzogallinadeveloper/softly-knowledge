## Glance
Staying active is safe and good in most pregnancies — gently, without exhausting yourself.

## Overview
Keeping active in pregnancy is good for you and isn't dangerous for your baby — in fact, staying fit
can help you cope with the changes ahead and with labour. You don't need to take up anything intense;
carrying on with your normal daily activity, as long as it feels comfortable, is a fine starting
point.

A simple rule of thumb: you should be able to **hold a conversation** as you move. If you're too
breathless to talk, ease off. Gentle options like walking, swimming, aqua-natal classes, and
pregnancy yoga suit most people. If you weren't active before, this isn't the time to begin a
strenuous new routine — build up gently instead.

A few activities are best avoided or approached with care — such as lying flat on your back after
around 16 weeks, contact sports, scuba diving, and anything with a real risk of falling.

## When to see a doctor
- Check with your **midwife** before starting a new aerobic exercise programme, or if you have a
  health condition or pregnancy concern.
- **Stop and get advice** if you feel unwell, dizzy or very breathless, or if you have any pain,
  bleeding, or contractions while exercising.
