## Glance
Period pain is cramping in your lower tummy around your period — common, and often a normal part of the cycle.

## Overview
Period pain is the cramping, aching feeling in your lower tummy that many people get around their
period. It happens when the muscular wall of the womb tightens, and it's often a normal part of the
menstrual cycle. The medical name for it is dysmenorrhea.

It usually comes on at the start of your period, or just before, and tends to last up to about
three days. For some it's a mild heaviness; for others the cramps are stronger. Pain can also
change over your life and from cycle to cycle.

Period pain that follows this familiar pattern is common and usually manageable. Everyday comfort
measures and simple painkillers are what many people use, and a pharmacist or GP can advise on what
might suit you. What matters is knowing your own usual pattern — so you can notice if something
changes.

## When to see a doctor
It's worth talking to a GP if:

- your periods become more painful, heavier, or more irregular than usual
- the pain stops you doing everyday things like work or school
- ordinary painkillers don't help
- you have pain during sex, or when peeing or pooing
- you bleed between periods
- your pain comes at times other than just before or during your period
- you pass blood clots larger than a coin (about the size of a quarter)
- you also notice a swollen tummy, less appetite, or weight loss you can't explain

These don't mean something is wrong, but they're worth checking — period pain that's new,
different, or hard to manage can have causes a clinician can help with.

## Red flags
- Ask for an **urgent** GP appointment or call **NHS 111** if your period or pelvic pain is
  **severe, or worse than usual, and painkillers have not helped.**
