## Glance
Premature labour is labour before 37 weeks — act quickly, as early care makes a real difference.

## Overview
Premature (or preterm) labour is labour that starts before the 37th week of pregnancy. It happens in
roughly 8 in 100 births.

Babies born early are more vulnerable, but many go on to do well — especially with the specialist
care that neonatal units provide. The key is acting quickly if you think it might be starting, so you
can be assessed and, if needed, given treatments that help protect the baby.

The signs are similar to labour at any stage, but before 37 weeks they should never be waited out —
they're always a reason to make contact straight away.

## When to see a doctor
- Call your **midwife or maternity unit** any time you're **less than 37 weeks** and unsure or
  worried — it's always okay to check.

## Red flags
Contact your **midwife or maternity unit immediately** — day or night — if, **before 37 weeks**, you
have any of these:

- regular **contractions or tightenings**, or period-type pains
- a **gush or trickle of fluid** from your vagina (your waters breaking)
- **backache** that's not usual for you, or a feeling of pressure low down
