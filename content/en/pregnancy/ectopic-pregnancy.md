## Glance
An ectopic pregnancy grows outside the womb — it can't continue and needs prompt medical care.

## Overview
An ectopic pregnancy is when a fertilised egg implants outside the womb, most often in one of the
fallopian tubes. Sadly, a pregnancy in this position can't develop into a baby, and it can't be
moved into the womb — so it always needs treating.

Signs usually show up between about weeks 4 and 12, and can include tummy pain low down on one side,
vaginal bleeding or a brown watery discharge, pain in the tip of your shoulder, or discomfort when
you pee or poo. The mix of symptoms varies, and some can be easy to mistake for something minor,
which is why getting them checked matters.

It's an uncommon situation, but a potentially serious one, so it's not something to wait out. Getting
advice early means it can be looked after safely.

## When to see a doctor
- Contact your GP or call **NHS 111** if you have a combination of these symptoms **and you might be
  pregnant** — even if you haven't had a positive pregnancy test. It's important to get advice right
  away.

## Red flags
- Call **999 or go to A&E** if you have a **sudden, sharp, intense pain in your tummy**, feel **very
  dizzy or faint**, look **very pale**, or feel sick with these. This can mean the fallopian tube
  has ruptured — a medical emergency that needs urgent treatment.
