## Glance
Most foods are fine in pregnancy — a few are worth avoiding, and it helps to keep caffeine low.

## Overview
The good news is that most foods are perfectly fine in pregnancy — only a small number are worth
avoiding or taking care with, mostly to steer clear of bugs like listeria or too much of certain
vitamins. Knowing the short list means you can relax about the rest.

The NHS advises taking care with:

- **Some cheeses** — mould-ripened soft cheeses like brie and camembert, and soft blue cheeses,
  unless they're cooked until steaming hot; and anything made with **unpasteurised** milk.
- **Raw or undercooked meat**, cold cured meats, **pâté**, and **liver** (which is very high in
  vitamin A).
- **Raw or runny eggs**, unless they carry the British Lion mark.
- **Some fish** — avoid shark, swordfish and marlin, limit tuna, and have no more than two portions
  of oily fish a week.
- **Caffeine** — keep it under about 200mg a day (roughly two mugs of instant coffee), counting tea,
  cola, energy drinks and chocolate too.

## When to see a doctor
- This is general guidance, not a strict diet — if you have questions about what's safe for you, or
  a specific dietary need, your **midwife** can talk it through.
- If you've eaten something on the "avoid" list, try not to panic; mention it to your midwife if
  you're worried or feel unwell.
