## Glance
Spotting is light bleeding between periods or after sex — often not serious, but always worth getting checked.

## Overview
Spotting means light bleeding that happens between your periods or after sex, outside your normal
flow. It's common, and it's usually not something serious — but because it can have several causes,
it's the kind of thing that's always worth getting checked by a clinician.

There are many everyday reasons for it. Starting or using hormonal contraception is a frequent one.
Others include small, harmless growths in or around the womb (polyps or fibroids), harmless changes
to the surface of the cervix, vaginal dryness, or an infection such as chlamydia.

Because the same symptom can come from different causes — most of them minor — the useful step
isn't to guess, it's to have it looked at so you know what's behind it for you.

## When to see a doctor
- See a GP or a sexual health clinic **any time you have bleeding between periods or after sex**,
  even if it's light. It's usually nothing serious, but getting it checked is how the cause is
  found — and it's the way to rule out less common causes early.

## Red flags
- Ask for an **urgent** GP appointment or call **NHS 111** if you have **missed a period and have
  unusual bleeding along with tummy or pelvic pain** — this needs prompt assessment, as it can be a
  sign of an ectopic pregnancy.
