## Glance
Sex on your period is fine if you both want it, but you can still get pregnant or pass on an STI.

## Overview
Having sex during your period is a personal choice. If you both want to and you both feel
comfortable, it's okay, and there's nothing wrong or unusual about it. Some people feel more
sensitive or simply not in the mood at this time, while others enjoy it. Listening to your body and
talking openly with your partner is what matters most.

What a period doesn't do is work as contraception. The chance of getting pregnant is lower than
around ovulation, but it isn't zero. Sperm can survive inside the body for up to 7 days after sex,
so if you ovulate early, which is more likely with a shorter cycle, sperm from sex during or just
after your period could still meet an egg. If you don't want to get pregnant, your usual
contraception still matters on these days.

A period doesn't protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs) either. Infections can pass
between partners during sex without a condom, and the germs that cause them can be carried in blood
as well as in other body fluids. Condoms are the method that also lowers the risk of STIs, including
HIV, so they're worth using whatever the time of the month.

## When to see a doctor
- If you've had sex **without contraception**, or your contraception may have failed, and you don't
  want to get pregnant, speak to a pharmacist, GP or sexual health clinic **as soon as you can**
  about emergency contraception. It needs to be used within a few days, and it usually works better
  the sooner it's used.
- Get help from a pharmacist, sexual health clinic or GP if a **condom splits or comes
  off** during sex. You may need emergency contraception or an STI test.
- See a GP or a sexual health clinic if you notice **bleeding after sex when you're not on your
  period**. It's usually not serious, but it's always worth getting checked.
