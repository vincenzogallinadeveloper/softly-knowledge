## Glance
Estrogen is a key hormone that helps prepare your body for a possible pregnancy each cycle.

## Overview
Estrogen is one of the main hormones behind the menstrual cycle. In the first half of the cycle
(the follicular phase) its level rises as a follicle in the ovary matures. This rise thickens the
lining of the uterus and leads up to ovulation.

Estrogen also influences things you might notice day to day — energy, mood, and changes in
cervical mucus around your fertile days. After ovulation its level dips and then shifts again,
working alongside another hormone, progesterone, through the second half of the cycle.

Levels naturally rise and fall across each cycle, and they change across life stages too. This is
normal — it's the rhythm your body runs on, not something to "fix."

## Deep
Estrogen isn't a single substance but a group of related hormones (the most active in the cycle
is estradiol). Most of it is made by the ovaries, in the growing follicle. Its cyclical rise and
fall — together with progesterone, and the brain's signalling hormones LH and FSH — is what
drives the phases of the cycle. Because estrogen touches many systems, its natural shifts can
show up in small ways throughout the month; tracking helps you learn your own pattern rather than
compare it to an "average."
