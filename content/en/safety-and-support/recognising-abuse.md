## Glance
Abuse can be physical, sexual, emotional or controlling — and it is never the fault of the person experiencing it.

## Overview
Abuse in a relationship isn't only physical. It can take several forms, often together: physical
harm, sexual coercion, emotional or psychological harm, and controlling behaviours — a partner
deciding where you go, who you see, or what you can do. Any of these can be abuse, even when there
is no visible injury.

It is more common than many people realise. Worldwide, around 1 in 3 women experience physical or
sexual violence in their lifetime, most often from a partner; the picture in Italy is similar. So if
this is part of your life, you are not alone, and it is not something you have caused.

Naming it can be hard — abuse often comes mixed with good times, apologies, or promises to change,
and it can build up so slowly that it feels normal. Trusting your own sense that something is wrong
is a valid reason to reach out. You do not have to be certain, or wait for it to get "bad enough,"
to talk to someone.

## Deep
Underneath the different forms, abuse tends to follow a pattern of one person seeking power and
control over another, and it can escalate over time. It takes a real toll on health — it is linked
with anxiety, depression, trouble sleeping, and long-term physical effects. None of that means you
are weak or to blame; it means what you are living with is harmful, and support exists to help you
think it through safely and at your own pace.

## When to see a doctor
- You can talk to someone in confidence whenever you're ready — a GP or nurse, an anti-violence
  centre, or a helpline (in Italy, **1522**, free and any time). You don't need proof, a plan, or a
  decision first; they are there to listen and to help you understand your options.
- A health professional can also treat any injuries and quietly connect you with specialist support.

## Red flags
- If you are in **immediate danger**, contact emergency services now: **112** in the EU (including
  Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
- In Italy, **1522** — the free national anti-violence and stalking helpline — is available 24 hours
  a day, anonymously, by phone or chat, in several languages.
