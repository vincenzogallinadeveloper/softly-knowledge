## Glance
Back pain is very common in pregnancy as ligaments soften and your bump grows — usually harmless.

## Overview
Back pain is one of the most common parts of pregnancy. As your body gets ready for birth, the
ligaments naturally soften and stretch, and your growing bump changes how you carry your weight —
both of which put extra strain on your lower back and pelvis.

Everyday things can ease it: being mindful of your posture when standing, sitting and lifting;
wearing flat, supportive shoes; a firm mattress or a supportive pillow; and gentle, regular movement
like walking, pregnancy yoga, or exercises from a class. If it's really getting in the way, an
obstetric physiotherapist can give tailored advice.

Most pregnancy back pain is nothing to worry about — but a few patterns are worth getting checked
promptly, which is what the guidance below is for.

## When to see a doctor
Contact your **GP or midwife** promptly if your back pain:

- is **severe**, or comes on in the **second or third trimester** (it can sometimes be a sign of
  early labour)
- comes with a **fever, vaginal bleeding, or pain when you pee**
- is **in your side(s), under your ribs**

## Red flags
- Call **999 or go to A&E** if you **lose feeling in your legs, buttocks, or genitals**, or lose
  control of your bladder or bowels. This is rare, but needs emergency care.
