## Glance
Free, confidential help is available — in an emergency call your local emergency number; in Italy, 1522 any time.

## Overview
If you are experiencing abuse, you don't have to face it alone, and reaching out doesn't commit you
to anything. There are people whose whole job is to listen and help, in confidence and for free.

A few kinds of help exist side by side:

- **Emergency services** — for immediate danger, call your local emergency number (below).
- **A national helpline** — in Italy, **1522**, the free anti-violence and stalking helpline: open 24
  hours a day, anonymous, available in several languages, by phone or online chat.
- **Anti-violence centres** (in Italy, *centri antiviolenza*) — local services offering free,
  confidential support, safety planning, and legal and psychological help.
- **Health services** — a GP, or a hospital emergency department, where staff are increasingly
  trained to recognise abuse and connect you with support.
- **People you trust** — a friend or family member you feel safe with.

You can start with whichever feels most possible, and you don't need to have decided anything.

## When to see a doctor
- You can speak to a **GP or a hospital** in confidence about what's happening; they can treat any
  injuries and put you in touch with specialist services.
- If it's easier to write than to talk, the **1522 chat** is another way in.

## Red flags
- If you are in **immediate danger**, contact emergency services now: **112** in the EU (including
  Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
- **Italy:** **1522** (free, 24/7, anonymous, multilingual — call or chat).
- Helpline numbers differ from country to country. The app can show the numbers for your country,
  and **findahelpline.com** lists anti-violence helplines worldwide (it detects your country and has
  a quick-exit button). If you are in the EU, **112** works everywhere.
