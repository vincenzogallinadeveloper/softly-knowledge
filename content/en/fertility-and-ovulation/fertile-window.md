## Glance
The fertile window is the handful of days each cycle when sex can lead to pregnancy.

## Overview
The fertile window is the short stretch of the cycle when pregnancy is possible. It exists because
of two timings that don't quite line up: an egg can only be fertilised for about 12 to 24 hours
after it's released, but sperm can survive inside the body for several days — up to about a week —
waiting for an egg.

Put together, that means the window opens a few days before ovulation and closes about a day
after it. In practice you're most fertile in the 2 to 3 days leading up to ovulation and on the
day itself, because sperm can already be there when the egg arrives.

Since ovulation can shift from cycle to cycle, the fertile window moves too. That's why it's
usually thought of as a range of days rather than a single date, and why tracking signs across
several cycles is more dependable than counting on one calendar calculation.

## Deep
The exact days differ from person to person and month to month, so the window is best estimated
from the body's own signals rather than a fixed formula. Changes in cervical mucus point to the
window opening as ovulation approaches, and a rise in resting body temperature confirms — after
the fact — that ovulation has passed and the window has closed. Ovulation predictor kits work a
step earlier, detecting the surge in luteinizing hormone (LH) that comes shortly before the egg is
released.
