## Glance
A home pregnancy test checks your urine for the pregnancy hormone hCG — most reliable from the first missed period.

## Overview
A home pregnancy test works by detecting a hormone called hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin) in
your urine. Your body starts making it a few days after a fertilised egg implants, and the amount
rises quickly in early pregnancy.

The test is most reliable from the **first day of a missed period** — roughly two weeks after
conception. If your cycle is irregular or you're not sure when your period is due, waiting until at
least 21 days after unprotected sex gives a more dependable result. You don't need to test first
thing in the morning; any time of day is fine.

A **positive** result is almost certainly right — very few tests show positive when you're not
pregnant. A **negative** result is a little less certain: if your period still doesn't arrive and you
think you might be pregnant, wait a few days and test again.

## When to see a doctor
- If you get a positive result, contact a **midwife or GP** to begin antenatal care.
- If tests are negative but your period is missing and you feel pregnant (or unwell), see a GP to
  find out what's happening.
