## Glance
FSH is a brain hormone that prompts follicles in the ovary to grow at the start of each cycle.

## Overview
FSH — follicle-stimulating hormone — is one of the messenger hormones that get the cycle going.
It's made by a small gland in the brain (the pituitary) and, as its name says, it stimulates
follicles: the tiny fluid-filled sacs in the ovary that each hold an egg.

Early in the cycle, FSH encourages a group of these follicles to start growing. As a follicle
develops it releases more estrogen, which in turn thickens the lining of the uterus (womb) ready
for a possible pregnancy. Usually one follicle becomes the dominant one that goes on toward
ovulation.

FSH rises and falls as part of the normal back-and-forth of the cycle. You don't feel FSH directly
— it works quietly in the background, setting up the more visible changes of the follicular phase.

## Deep
FSH works as a pair with luteinizing hormone (LH); both are released by the pituitary gland in the
brain and together they drive the ovaries through the cycle. FSH leads the first half, growing the
follicles, while the LH surge later marks the mid-cycle release of an egg. This partnership is also
why fertility treatments use FSH-based medicines: giving extra FSH encourages several follicles to
mature at once, rather than the single one a natural cycle usually produces.
