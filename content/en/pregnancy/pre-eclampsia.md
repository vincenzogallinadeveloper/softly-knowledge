## Glance
Pre-eclampsia is raised blood pressure in pregnancy — often silent at first, which is why antenatal checks matter.

## Overview
Pre-eclampsia is a condition of pregnancy that mainly shows up as high blood pressure, along with
protein in the urine. It usually appears from around 20 weeks, and sometimes after the birth.

One of the most important things to know is that early on it often causes **no symptoms at all** —
you can feel completely fine. That's exactly why your midwife checks your blood pressure and tests
your urine at every routine antenatal appointment: those checks are how it's usually picked up
before you'd notice anything.

When symptoms do come, they're worth knowing, because pre-eclampsia can become serious for both you
and your baby. It's very treatable when caught, and keeping up with your antenatal appointments is
the single best way to stay ahead of it.

## When to see a doctor
- Keep all your **routine antenatal appointments** — the blood-pressure and urine checks are how
  pre-eclampsia is usually found before symptoms start.

## Red flags
Get medical help **immediately** — contact your maternity unit, or call **NHS 111** — if you have
any of these (call **999** if you're seriously unwell, for example a fit/seizure):

- a **severe headache** that doesn't ease with ordinary painkillers
- **vision problems** — blurring or flashing lights
- **sudden swelling** of your face, hands, or feet
- **pain just below your ribs**
- being **sick**, or suddenly feeling very unwell
