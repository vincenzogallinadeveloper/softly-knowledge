## Glance
Love is built on respect and freedom — control, jealousy and pressure are not love, and help exists.

## Overview
A healthy relationship rests on respect, trust, equality and freedom: two people who each keep their
own friendships, choices and space. Control is the opposite. It often hides behind words like love or
jealousy — "I check your phone because I care", "I don't want you seeing them because I love you" — but
its effect is to shrink her world and her freedom.

Control rarely starts with violence. It usually begins small — a criticism, a boundary crossed, a
"joke" that stings — and grows. Naming it early matters, because coercive control is itself a form of
abuse and a warning sign for worse. This is not about guilt; it's about honesty and change.

## Deep
**If you recognise some of these in yourself, that awareness is a starting point — not a life sentence.**
People can change with real effort and the right help. Reaching out to a dedicated programme for men
who want to stop being controlling or violent is a sign of strength, not weakness. Talk to a
professional, a doctor, or a men's behaviour-change service.

If **she** is the one in danger, or you're worried for someone, encourage and support her to reach
help — on her terms, never by taking over.

**Where to get help**
- In an emergency, or if anyone is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number
  (**112** in Italy and much of Europe; **911** in the US).
- **Italy — 1522**: the national anti-violence and stalking helpline. Free, 24/7, anonymous, and
  multilingual, run by the Department for Equal Opportunities. It also guides people to local
  anti-violence centres (centri antiviolenza).
- Anti-violence centres and men's behaviour-change programmes (in Italy, "centri per uomini autori di
  violenza") offer confidential support to change.

## Red flags
Warning signs of controlling or abusive behaviour — worth taking seriously in yourself or a relationship:

- **Monitoring or isolating**: checking her phone, tracking her location, or cutting her off from
  friends and family.
- **Control over choices**: money, what she wears, where she goes, who she sees.
- **Jealousy framed as love**, constant accusations, or needing to know where she is at all times.
- **Put-downs, blame, or making her feel afraid**, guilty, or "walking on eggshells".
- **Pressure or threats** about sex, breaking up, or self-harm to get compliance.
- **Any physical force, intimidation, or fear.** If this is present, it is abuse — reach the help
  above. There is never an excuse, and it is never her fault.
