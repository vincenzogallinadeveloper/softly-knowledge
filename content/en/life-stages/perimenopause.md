## Glance
Perimenopause is the transition leading up to menopause, when periods and hormones start to change.

## Overview
Perimenopause is the stretch of time leading up to menopause — the "around menopause" years. It
begins when you first notice the changes and runs until a full year after your last period, at
which point you've reached menopause. It can last several years.

During this time the ovaries gradually make less estrogen, and levels rise and fall less
predictably than before. The most common early sign is that periods change — becoming closer
together or further apart, lighter or heavier. Many of the symptoms linked with menopause, such as
hot flushes, sleep changes, or shifts in mood, can begin here while you're still having periods.

Because it's gradual and varies so much, perimenopause can be confusing — the changes don't arrive
all at once or in the same order for everyone. Knowing this phase has a name, and that it's a
normal transition, can make it easier to make sense of.

## When to see a doctor
- See a GP if perimenopause symptoms are affecting your daily life and you'd like to talk through
  what might help.
- It's worth getting checked if your periods become very heavy, or if you have bleeding between
  periods or after sex, so the cause can be found.
- Remember that pregnancy can still happen during perimenopause, so it's worth raising
  contraception with a clinician if that's relevant for you.
