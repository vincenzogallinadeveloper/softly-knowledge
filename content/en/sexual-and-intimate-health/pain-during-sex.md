## Glance
Pain during sex is common, has many possible causes, and is always worth getting checked — help exists.

## Overview
Pain during sex — sometimes called dyspareunia — is more common than many people realise, and it's
never something you simply have to accept. It can have many causes, and finding the right one is the
key to easing it.

Some causes are physical. Vaginal dryness is a frequent one, often linked to hormones around
menopause or to not enough arousal, and it can make sex feel sore or uncomfortable. Infections, such
as thrush, can also cause pain. Another cause is vaginismus — when the muscles around the vagina
tighten on their own as penetration is attempted, which can make sex painful or feel impossible.
This is involuntary, it's not "in your head," and it's treatable.

Because the cause can be physical, hormonal, or linked to how relaxed or aroused you feel — and
often a mix — the useful step is to have it looked at rather than to guess or to soldier on.

## When to see a doctor
- See a GP or a sexual health clinic if sex is painful — whatever the cause, it's worth checking,
  and there's effective help for the common causes.
- It can feel awkward to raise, but clinicians deal with this often and are there to help, not to
  judge. You can ask for a female doctor, bring someone with you, and talk through what would make
  an examination comfortable.
- Do get any bleeding after sex checked, and see someone sooner if the pain is severe or you also
  feel unwell.
