## Glance
Most of the time she needs to be heard, not fixed — listen first, and ask before you advise.

## Overview
When someone we love is struggling, the instinct is to jump in and solve it. But often what helps most
is feeling genuinely heard: that her feelings make sense and she isn't alone with them. Fixing too
fast can land as "your feelings are a problem to close down".

Good listening is a skill anyone can learn. It's mostly attention: putting the phone down, facing her,
and letting her finish. You don't have to agree with every point to make her feel respected.

## Deep
Practical ways to show up:

- **Listen to understand, not to reply.** Let her talk without interrupting or planning your defence.
- **Reflect it back.** "That sounds really stressful" or "I get why that hurt" beats "just don't
  worry about it".
- **Ask before advising.** "Do you want help thinking it through, or do you just want me to listen?"
- **Don't take it personally** when a hard day isn't about you. Steadiness is a gift.
- **Own your part.** If you got something wrong, a real apology — without "but" — rebuilds trust.
- **Notice the bigger picture.** If she seems persistently low, anxious or not herself, gently
  encourage support and offer to help her reach it. You're a teammate, not a therapist.
