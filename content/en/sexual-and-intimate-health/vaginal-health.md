## Glance
The vagina cleans itself — gentle care on the outside is all it needs, and discharge is normal.

## Overview
One of the most reassuring things to know is that the vagina looks after itself. It's naturally
self-cleaning, and the discharge you notice is part of that — a fluid that keeps it clean and moist
and helps protect it from infection. The amount and texture change through the cycle, which is
completely normal.

Because of this, the vagina doesn't need washing on the inside. In fact, washing inside it —
"douching" — can upset its natural balance. The outside (the vulva) just needs gentle care: warm
water and, if you like, a mild, non-perfumed soap. Perfumed soaps, gels, deodorants and scented
wipes are best avoided, as they can cause irritation.

Getting to know what's normal for you — your usual discharge, look and feel — makes it easier to
notice if something changes and might need checking.

## When to see a doctor
See a GP or sexual health clinic if you notice:

- a change in your discharge — its colour, smell, or texture, or a lot more than usual
- itching, soreness, or irritation around the vagina or vulva
- bleeding between periods or after sex
- pain when peeing, or pain low down between your tummy and thighs (pelvic pain)

These are common and often easily treated — a change in discharge can simply mean a mild infection
like thrush. Getting it checked is the way to know, rather than guessing.
