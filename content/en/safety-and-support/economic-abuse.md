## Glance
Economic abuse is controlling someone's access to money, work or resources — a recognised form of abuse.

## Overview
Economic abuse is when someone controls another person's access to money and the things money buys.
It's a form of controlling behaviour, and it often sits alongside other kinds of abuse.

It can look like controlling all the spending or making you account for every penny, taking your
wages or benefits, running up debt in your name, keeping bank accounts or documents from you, or
stopping you from working or studying. Because it can leave a person without the means to leave or to
manage day to day, it's one of the ways abuse can feel like a trap.

If some of this is familiar, it is not a sign that you are "bad with money" or to blame. Controlling
another person's resources is a choice the other person makes — and it is recognised as abuse.

## When to see a doctor
- Anti-violence services can help with this too — not only safety, but practical and financial steps,
  in confidence and free of charge (in Italy, the **1522** helpline can point you to local support).
- A safety plan can include quietly securing access to some money, key documents, and important
  numbers.

## Red flags
- If you are in **immediate danger**, contact emergency services now: **112** in the EU (including
  Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
- In Italy, **1522** — free, 24 hours a day, anonymous, multilingual — can help by phone or chat.
