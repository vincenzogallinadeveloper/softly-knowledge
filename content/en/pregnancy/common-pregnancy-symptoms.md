## Glance
Pregnancy brings lots of small aches and changes — mostly normal, though a few are worth checking.

## Overview
Pregnancy is a lot of change for your body, and it comes with a long list of everyday niggles. Most
are common and not a sign that anything is wrong — they're just part of growing a baby.

Familiar ones include backache, heartburn and indigestion, constipation, piles, swollen ankles, feet
and fingers, tiredness and broken sleep, leg cramp, headaches, feeling faint, and more discharge than
usual. Many ease with small comfort measures, and your midwife can suggest what's safe if something's
bothering you.

The useful thing is knowing which changes are the ordinary kind and which ones are worth flagging —
because a few symptoms, though less common, do need checking rather than waiting out.

## When to see a doctor
Most of these are normal, but contact your **midwife or maternity unit** promptly if you have:

- **vaginal bleeding**, or a sudden gush or trickle of fluid
- your **baby moving less** than usual
- a **severe headache, vision problems, or sudden swelling** of the face, hands or feet (possible
  signs of pre-eclampsia)
- **severe or one-sided tummy pain**, or a **high temperature**
- **itching**, especially on your hands and feet or that's worse at night (it can be a sign of a
  liver condition in pregnancy that needs checking)
