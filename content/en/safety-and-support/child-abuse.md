## Glance
If you're worried a child may be being harmed or neglected, it is right to seek advice — you don't have to be certain.

## Overview
Child abuse — or child maltreatment — is harm to anyone under 18. It includes physical, sexual, and
emotional abuse, and neglect (not meeting a child's basic needs). It is sadly common, it can cause
lifelong harm, and it is never the child's fault.

You might have a worry about a child in your life, or about a child you've noticed. You don't have to
be sure, or to have proof, to act on that worry — trusting it and asking for advice is exactly the
right thing to do, and it can protect a child.

And if you yourself experienced abuse as a child, know that the harm was never your fault, and that
support is available for you too, at any age.

## When to see a doctor
- If you're worried about a child, you can contact **child protection / social services**, a
  dedicated **child-protection helpline**, or a **GP, health visitor, or the child's school** for
  advice — in confidence.
- You are not "overreacting" by asking. It's better to check than to wait.

## Red flags
- If a child is in **immediate danger** or seriously hurt, contact emergency services now: **112** in
  the EU (including Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
