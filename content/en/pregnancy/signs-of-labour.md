## Glance
Signs that labour is starting include regular contractions, a "show," backache, or your waters breaking.

## Overview
There are a few signs that labour may be starting. The main one is contractions — tightenings across
your bump that come regularly and build up. Others include a "show" (the plug of mucus from your
cervix coming away), a persistent backache, a need to go to the toilet, and your waters breaking.

Before active labour there's often an early ("latent") phase, when the cervix softens and starts to
open. This part can take hours or even days, and for many people it's fine to stay comfortable at
home during it, resting and keeping hydrated.

Everyone's labour begins a little differently, so you don't need to tick off every sign. When you
think things are starting, your midwife or maternity unit is there to guide you on what to do and
when to come in.

## When to see a doctor
- Call your **midwife or maternity unit** for guidance when you think labour has started, or when
  contractions are **regular — about every 5 minutes**. If you can't reach your midwife, call
  **NHS 111**.

## Red flags
Call your **midwife or maternity unit immediately** — day or night, don't wait — if:

- your **waters break** (especially if the fluid is green, brown, or smelly)
- you have **any vaginal bleeding**
- your **baby is moving less than usual**
- you have signs of labour and you're **less than 37 weeks pregnant**
