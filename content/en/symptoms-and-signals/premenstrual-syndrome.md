## Glance
PMS is the mix of physical and emotional changes many people notice in the week or two before a period.

## Overview
PMS — premenstrual syndrome — is the name for the changes many people feel in the days or couple of
weeks before their period. It can affect anyone who has periods, and it usually eases within a few
days after the period starts.

The changes come in many forms. Physically, you might notice bloating, sore or tender breasts,
cramps, headaches, or feeling more tired. Emotionally, there can be mood swings, irritability,
anxiety, or feeling low. Food cravings and changes in sleep are common too. Not everyone gets PMS,
and those who do won't get every symptom — your own pattern is what matters.

PMS is a normal, recognised part of many people's cycles, tied to the natural hormonal shifts of
the second half. Noticing how it shows up for you, cycle to cycle, can make it easier to plan
around — and to spot if it ever changes.

## When to see a doctor
It's worth talking to a GP if:

- your symptoms are affecting your daily life — work, studies, relationships
- everyday changes to your routine haven't helped
- your low mood, anxiety, or irritability feels severe

A clinician can talk through what might help. A more intense form, called premenstrual dysphoric
disorder (PMDD), can cause much stronger mood symptoms, and support is available for it.

## Red flags
- If you have symptoms of PMDD and are **feeling suicidal or that you cannot keep yourself safe**,
  get help straight away: **call 999 or go to A&E.** Don't drive yourself — ask someone to take
  you, or ask for an ambulance. You deserve support, and help is available.
