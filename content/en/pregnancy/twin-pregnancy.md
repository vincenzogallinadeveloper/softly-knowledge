## Glance

## Overview
Expecting more than one baby is usually first discovered at the **dating scan** (around 12 weeks),
which can also show whether the babies share a placenta.

There are two main kinds. **Identical** twins come from a single fertilised egg that splits, so they
share the same genes and are always the same sex. **Non-identical** twins come from two separate
eggs, and are no more alike than any other siblings; they're the more common of the two, and are a
bit more likely with older age, a family history on the mother's side, or after fertility treatment.

Carrying twins or more is its own experience, and it usually means a **little more antenatal care** —
extra appointments and scans to keep a close eye on you and your babies. Your midwifery team will
explain what to expect.

## When to see a doctor
- If a scan shows twins (or more), your **midwife** will talk you through the extra check-ups and
  scans that come with a multiple pregnancy — going along to them all is the best way to stay well
  looked after.
