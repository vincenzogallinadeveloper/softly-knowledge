## Glance
The implant is a tiny rod placed in the upper arm that releases a hormone for a few years.

## Overview
The contraceptive implant is a small, flexible rod — about the size of a matchstick — placed just
under the skin of the upper arm by a trained clinician. Once in, it steadily releases a progestogen
hormone and works for up to about three years.

It prevents pregnancy mainly by stopping the ovaries from releasing an egg (ovulation), and by
thickening the mucus at the neck of the womb so sperm can't get through. Because there's nothing to
remember day to day, it's one of the "fit and forget" methods.

That's also why it's one of the most reliable options: the implant is over 99% effective, and its
effectiveness barely changes between perfect and typical use. Like other hormonal methods, it
doesn't protect against STIs.

## Deep
The implant is a long-acting reversible method — it keeps working until it's removed, and fertility
returns after removal. Having it fitted and taken out are quick procedures done by a clinician, who
also talks through who it suits and its side effects. This atom explains what it is and how it
works; the decision to have one is a conversation with a GP, nurse, or sexual health clinic.
