## Glance
Postnatal depression is a common, treatable low mood after birth — it's not your fault, and help is there.

## Overview
Feeling down, anxious, or irritable in the first couple of weeks after birth is very common — this
is often called the "baby blues," and it usually passes on its own within about two weeks.

Postnatal depression is different: a low mood that's deeper or lasts longer. It's a common condition,
it can start any time in the first year, and it can affect fathers and partners too. Signs include a
persistent low mood, finding it hard to enjoy anything, trouble sleeping even when your baby sleeps,
and sometimes difficult feelings about bonding with your baby.

Having it does **not** mean you're a bad parent or that you don't love your baby — it's an illness,
not a failing, and it responds well to support and treatment. Reaching out early is a strong, caring
thing to do, for you and your baby.

## When to see a doctor
- Speak to your **GP, midwife, or health visitor** if you think you might have postnatal depression,
  or if the "baby blues" haven't lifted after two weeks. They will have seen it many times and can
  help you find the right support.

## Red flags
- If you have **thoughts of harming yourself or your baby**, or that your family would be better off
  without you, get help **right away**: call **999** or go to A&E, or call **NHS 111** or the
  Samaritans on **116 123**. These thoughts can be part of the illness, they are not who you are, and
  urgent help is available.
