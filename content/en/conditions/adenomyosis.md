## Glance
Adenomyosis is when the lining of the womb grows into the muscle of the womb's wall. It can cause heavy, painful periods.

## Overview
Adenomyosis is a condition where the tissue that normally lines the womb (uterus) grows into the
muscle in the wall of the womb. It's more often diagnosed in women over 30, and in people who have
given birth, but it can affect anyone who has periods. Its cause isn't clear.

The most common signs are severe period pain — sometimes bad enough to stop you doing your usual
things — and heavy periods. Some people also have pelvic pain (in the lower tummy) at other times of
the month, a bloated or heavy, full feeling in the tummy, or pain during sex. Others have no symptoms
at all.

It's a different condition from endometriosis, where tissue similar to the womb lining grows outside
the womb, for example on the ovaries. Some of their symptoms overlap, which is one reason tests can be
needed to tell what's causing the pain.

## Deep
A GP will usually ask about your periods and may gently feel your tummy; an internal examination may
be suggested, and you can ask for a female doctor or for someone to be with you. An ultrasound or an
MRI scan can help find adenomyosis or rule out other causes.

Treatments can help with the symptoms. Options a clinician may discuss include the hormonal coil
(IUS), which thins the womb lining and can make periods lighter and less painful; other hormonal
contraception, such as the combined pill, the progestogen-only pill or the patch; non-hormonal
medicines for heavy bleeding or pain; and, if these don't help, surgery. Because hormonal methods can
make periods lighter or stop them, some people take the pill with fewer breaks or none — see "Taking
the pill without breaks."

Heat on the tummy, a TENS machine and everyday painkillers can take the edge off the pain. Keeping a
simple record of your pain, bleeding and other symptoms over time helps you and your doctor see how
things are going and whether a treatment is working.

## When to see a doctor
It's worth seeing a GP if:

- your periods become more painful, heavier or irregular
- heavy periods are affecting your life, or have gone on for some time
- you have pain during sex
- you've felt bloated for about 3 weeks
- you bleed between periods or after sex

Ask for an urgent appointment, or contact an out-of-hours doctor, if your pelvic or period pain is
severe or worse than usual and painkillers haven't helped.

## Red flags
- Get **urgent** medical help — contact a doctor or an **out-of-hours service**, or call **{{emergency}}** / go to the emergency department if it's severe — if you're
  **bleeding so heavily you soak through a pad or more every hour for a few hours in a row**, or you
  feel **faint, dizzy, or short of breath**. These can be signs of losing too much blood.
