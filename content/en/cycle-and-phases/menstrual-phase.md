## Glance
Your period is the few days of bleeding that begin each cycle, as the womb lining is shed.

## Overview
The menstrual phase is your period — the days when you bleed. It starts on day 1 of the cycle,
when the levels of estrogen and progesterone have dropped low enough that the lining built up
inside the uterus (womb) is no longer maintained and comes away, leaving the body through the
vagina.

A period usually lasts about 2 to 7 days, with roughly 5 days being typical. Flow is often
heaviest in the first couple of days and lighter after that. Periods commonly begin around age 12,
though starting earlier or later is also normal.

What's "normal" varies a lot from person to person. Tracking your own periods helps you notice
what's usual for you, which is far more meaningful than any average.

## Deep
The bleeding of a period is the lining of the uterus (the endometrium) being shed after a cycle
where no pregnancy has begun. It marks both the end of one cycle and the start of the next, which
is why day 1 of your period is counted as day 1 of the whole cycle. As your period finishes,
estrogen begins to rise again and the body starts rebuilding the lining for the cycle ahead.
