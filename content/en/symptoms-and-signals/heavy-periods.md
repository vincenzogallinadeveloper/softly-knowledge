## Glance
Heavy periods mean losing a lot of blood each cycle — common, but worth checking if it affects you.

## Overview
Heavy periods — the medical name is menorrhagia — means losing a lot of blood during your periods.
For some people heavy bleeding is simply their normal, but if it's disrupting your life or leaving
you drained, it's worth looking into.

Some signs that periods are on the heavy side: needing to change a pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours,
using two types of period product together (like a tampon and a pad), bleeding through to your
clothes or bedding, passing blood clots larger than about 2.5cm (roughly a 10p coin), or periods
that last more than 7 days.

Losing a lot of blood month after month can leave you tired or short of breath, because it can lead
to low iron (anaemia). There are many possible causes — from fibroids or conditions like
endometriosis, adenomyosis or PCOS, to some medicines — and often no serious cause is found. Either
way, heavy periods can usually be helped, so they're not something you have to just live with.

## When to see a doctor
See a GP if:

- heavy periods are affecting your everyday life
- you're bleeding heavily for many hours, or your periods last a long time
- you also have severe period pain, or pain during sex, peeing, or pooing
- you have bleeding between periods or after sex
- you often feel tired or short of breath (this can be a sign of low iron)

A GP can look for a cause and talk through what might help.

## Red flags
- Get **urgent** medical help — call **NHS 111**, or **999 / go to A&E** if it's severe — if you're
  **bleeding so heavily you soak through a pad or more every hour for a few hours in a row**, or you
  feel **faint, dizzy, or short of breath**. These can be signs of losing too much blood.
