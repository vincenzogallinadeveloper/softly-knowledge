## Glance
Breast milk protects a baby from infections and adapts as they grow — and any amount has benefits.

## Overview
Breast milk is a natural, complete food for a baby: it provides the nutrition, vitamins and minerals
they need, and it changes over time to match their stage. It also helps protect against infections
and illnesses, and offers some longer-term health benefits.

There are benefits for the person feeding, too — breastfeeding is linked with a lower risk of breast
and ovarian cancer, among other things — and the general pattern is that the more and longer you
breastfeed, the greater the benefits. Importantly, **any** amount of breast milk is worthwhile; it's
not all-or-nothing.

Feeding a baby can also be harder than expected at first, and that's common. Help is available — from
your midwife, health visitor, and NHS feeding support — so reaching out early if it's not going
smoothly is a good move.

## When to see a doctor
- Ask your **midwife or health visitor** for help early if feeding is painful or difficult, or
  you're worried your baby isn't getting enough — support can make a real difference.
- How you feed your baby is a personal decision, and support is there whatever you choose; what
  matters is that you and your baby are well.
