## Glance
Bleeding in pregnancy isn't always serious — light spotting is common early on — but always get it checked.

## Overview
Bleeding from the vagina during pregnancy is not always a sign that something is wrong. Light
bleeding or spotting is fairly common in early pregnancy in particular, and many pregnancies continue
perfectly well after it.

That said, bleeding can sometimes be a sign of a problem — such as a miscarriage or an ectopic
pregnancy — so it's the kind of thing that should **always be checked**, whatever the amount. Getting
it looked at is how the cause is found and how you get the right care quickly if you need it.

Because the same symptom can mean very different things, the useful step isn't to guess — it's to
make contact and be seen.

## When to see a doctor
- **Always contact your maternity unit** (or, if you're less than 20 weeks or don't have their
  number, an early pregnancy unit or **NHS 111**) if you have **any** vaginal bleeding in pregnancy,
  even light spotting — so the cause can be checked.

## Red flags
- Call **999 or go to A&E** if you have **heavy bleeding** — for example soaking a pad soon after
  putting it on — especially with **severe tummy pain, shoulder-tip pain, or feeling sick, faint,
  dizzy, or losing consciousness.** This needs emergency care.
