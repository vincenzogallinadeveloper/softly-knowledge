## Glance
Sexual violence is any sexual act without consent — and it is never the fault of the person who experienced it.

## Overview
Sexual violence means any sexual act, or attempt to obtain one, without consent or through coercion.
It includes rape and any unwanted sexual contact, and it can be committed by anyone — a stranger, an
acquaintance, or a partner or spouse.

Consent is at the heart of it. Consent means freely agreeing, and it isn't possible when someone is
asleep, unconscious, drunk or drugged, a child, or pressured, threatened, or afraid. It can be
withdrawn at any point. Because of this, sexual violence is **never** the fault of the person it
happened to — not because of what they wore, whether they had been drinking, whether they froze
rather than fought, or whether they knew the person.

If this has happened to you, support is there, whether it was recent or long ago. You are believed,
and what you do next is your choice.

## When to see a doctor
- You can go to a **health service, a hospital, or a specialised sexual-assault service**, which can
  care for you, treat any injuries, and — if you wish — help with reporting and keeping evidence.
- Some health steps are **time-sensitive** — for example emergency contraception, or preventing
  infection — so reaching out **soon** can help. But there is no time limit on getting support, and
  it's always your choice whether to report.

## Red flags
- If you are in **immediate danger**, contact emergency services now: **112** in the EU (including
  Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
- After a recent assault, a **hospital or specialised service can help urgently**. In Italy, **1522**
  (free, 24/7, anonymous, multilingual — phone or chat) can guide you to the right care.
