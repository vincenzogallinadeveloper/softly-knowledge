## Glance
A safety plan is a few practical steps to help you stay safer — and trained services can help you make one.

## Overview
A safety plan is simply thinking ahead about how to keep yourself (and any children) safer. It isn't
a decision to leave or to stay — it's about being a little more prepared, whatever you choose.

For some people it helps to have a few things quietly in place: a trusted person who knows what's
happening, important documents and numbers where you can reach them, a small bag of essentials, a
sense of the safest ways out of the home, or a word or signal that tells someone you need help. What
makes sense depends entirely on your own situation.

You don't have to work this out alone, and it's often safer not to. Anti-violence services are
experienced at making safety plans with people, confidentially and free — including for the time
around leaving, which can be a higher-risk moment and is worth planning with support rather than in a
hurry.

## When to see a doctor
- Contact an anti-violence centre or a helpline (in Italy, **1522**) to make a plan that fits your
  situation — they do this every day, in confidence, and can help with practical, legal and
  emotional steps.
- If you're keeping any record of what's happening, keep it somewhere private and safe.

## Red flags
- If you are in **immediate danger**, contact emergency services now: **112** in the EU (including
  Italy), **999** in the UK, **911** in the US and Canada.
- In Italy, **1522** — free, 24 hours a day, anonymous, multilingual — can help by phone or chat,
  including with urgent safety planning.
