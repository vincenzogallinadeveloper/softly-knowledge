## Glance
Breast awareness is simply knowing how your breasts normally look and feel, so you notice changes.

## Overview
Breast awareness isn't a special technique — it's just getting to know what's normal for you, so
that anything new stands out. Checking your breasts or chest regularly helps you learn their usual
look and feel; once a month is a good rhythm.

There's no single "right" way. Many people look in the mirror with arms down and then raised, and
feel each breast, up into the armpit. What you're building is familiarity, not a diagnosis — the
goal is just to notice a change early if one happens.

Breasts also change naturally across the cycle and through life, so some changes are completely
normal. The point of awareness is knowing your own normal well enough to spot something that isn't.

## When to see a doctor
See a GP if you notice any change that isn't normal for you, such as:

- a lump or swelling in your breast, chest, or armpit
- a change in the skin, like dimpling (it can look like orange peel) or redness
- a change in the size or shape of one or both breasts
- nipple discharge (if you're not pregnant or breastfeeding), which may have blood in it
- a change in the look of your nipple, such as it turning inwards
- pain in your breast or armpit that doesn't go away

Most breast changes are not cancer and turn out to be harmless — but it's always worth getting a
change checked promptly, because if something does need attention, finding it early helps.
