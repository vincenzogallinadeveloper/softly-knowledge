## Glance
Antenatal care is the regular check-ups through pregnancy that help keep you and your baby well.

## Overview
Antenatal care is the care you get during pregnancy to keep you and your baby as healthy as
possible. You'll usually see a midwife, and a doctor too if you need one. It's worth starting early:
contact your midwife or GP as soon as you know you're pregnant.

Your first proper appointment — the "booking appointment" — is usually between 8 and 12 weeks. After
that you're offered regular check-ups: at least 10 appointments and 2 ultrasound scans if it's your
first baby, or around 7 if you've given birth before, with more towards the end.

At these visits your midwife checks your blood pressure and urine, measures your bump to follow your
baby's growth, offers screening tests, and talks with you about your baby's movements and how you're
feeling. Many important things — like pre-eclampsia or gestational diabetes — are picked up here
before you'd notice anything, which is exactly why the appointments matter.

## When to see a doctor
- Contact a **midwife or GP as soon as you find out you're pregnant** so your care can start early —
  ideally in time for that first appointment before around 10 to 12 weeks.
- Try to keep **all** your antenatal appointments, even if you feel completely well — several checks
  are designed to catch silent problems early.
