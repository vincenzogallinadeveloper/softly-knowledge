## Glance
Your first period usually arrives around age 12 — anywhere from about 8 to 17 is normal.

## Overview
Your first period — sometimes called menarche — is when the menstrual cycle begins for the first
time. Most people start around age 12, but anywhere from about 8 to 17 is normal. It often comes
roughly two years after your breasts start to develop.

There are usually signs in the lead-up: growing underarm and pubic hair, and a little clear or
white discharge in the months before. Knowing these are coming can make the first period feel less
sudden.

At the start, periods are often irregular — the gaps can be long or short, and it can take your
body several months, even a couple of years, to settle into a steadier rhythm. Once it does,
periods tend to come somewhere around every 23 to 35 days and last about 2 to 7 days. Early
irregularity is normal and not something to worry about.

## When to see a doctor
- See a GP if periods **have not started by age 15**, or by **13 if there are no other signs of
  puberty** (like breast development or body hair). This is just to check things are developing as
  expected — usually everything is fine.
