## Glance
Basal body temperature is your at-rest morning temperature, which rises slightly after ovulation.

## Overview
Basal body temperature (BBT) is your body's temperature fully at rest. Measured the same way each
day, it shows a small but telling change across the cycle: after ovulation, it rises a little and
stays up until your next period.

The rise is small — around 0.4 to 0.8°F (roughly 0.2 to 0.4°C) — so it's read from a pattern over
several days, not a single reading. To catch it, you take your temperature each morning before
getting up, eating or drinking, ideally at the same time. Almost everyone has ovulated within about
three days of the temperature climbing.

Because the rise comes *after* the egg is released, BBT confirms that ovulation has happened rather
than predicting it in advance. Over a few cycles it can help you see the shape of your own cycle,
though on its own it isn't a precise way to catch the fertile days.

## Deep
BBT is one of the fertility-awareness signs recorded daily, together with cervical mucus and cycle
length. It pairs naturally with mucus tracking: the change to clear, slippery mucus suggests the
fertile window is opening, while the temperature rise a few days later confirms ovulation has
passed. Many things can nudge a resting temperature — poor sleep, illness, alcohol, or a lie-in —
so single odd readings matter less than the overall trend.
