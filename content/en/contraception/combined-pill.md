## Glance
The combined pill is a daily pill with two hormones that mainly works by stopping ovulation.

## Overview
The combined pill is a daily tablet containing two hormones — versions of estrogen and progestogen,
similar to the ones the ovaries make. It's one of the most familiar forms of contraception.

It prevents pregnancy in a few ways at once. Mainly, it stops the ovaries from releasing an egg
(ovulation). It also thickens the mucus at the neck of the womb, making it harder for sperm to get
through, and thins the lining of the womb.

How well it works depends on taking it as directed: it's over 99% effective with perfect use, but
with typical, everyday use — where pills are sometimes missed or delayed — it's around 91%
effective. Like most methods, it doesn't protect against STIs, so some people use condoms as well.

## Deep
The combined pill is usually taken in a pattern across the month, and how to take it, who it suits,
and its side effects and risks are things a clinician goes through when it's prescribed — it's not
suitable for everyone. Because it's a daily method, its real-world effectiveness leans a lot on
routine. This atom explains what it is and how it works; the specifics of starting, taking, or
stopping it are a conversation with a GP, nurse, or pharmacist.
