## Glance
Sex is safe in most pregnancies and won't hurt the baby, unless your midwife or doctor has advised otherwise.

## Overview
For most people, having sex during pregnancy is safe, unless your midwife or doctor has told you
not to. The baby is well protected inside the womb, and sex won't harm them.

You may be advised to avoid penetrative sex in a few situations: if you have a **low-lying placenta
(placenta praevia)**, because of the risk of bleeding; if your **waters have broken**, because of
the risk of infection; or if you have had **several miscarriages**.

Desire often changes in pregnancy — some people want sex more, some less, some not at all for a
while — with hormones, body changes, tiredness, sickness and worries all playing a part. All of this
is normal. If you don't feel like sex, kissing, cuddling and massage are other ways to stay close.

As your bump grows, some positions become uncomfortable; side-by-side positions often feel easier.
Hormones can make the vagina drier, and a water-based lubricant can help.

Late in pregnancy, sex or an orgasm can bring on Braxton Hicks tightenings, when the womb goes hard
for a while. That's normal and isn't labour starting — and there's no evidence that sex brings
labour on.

If either of you might have a sexually transmitted infection, use a condom, including for oral
sex: an untreated infection can seriously harm you and the baby. Avoid oral sex if either of you
has a cold sore, as the virus can be passed to the baby.

## When to see a doctor
- Talk to your **midwife or doctor** if you're not sure sex is safe for you, or if worries about sex
  or your relationship are weighing on you.
- If you have **any bleeding** in pregnancy, get it checked before having sex again — contact your
  midwife, doctor or maternity unit.
- Go to a **sexual health clinic** if you or your partner might have an STI.

## Red flags
- Contact your **maternity unit** straight away if you think your **waters have broken**, and don't
  have sex until you've been seen.
- Call **{{emergency}} or go to the emergency department** if you have **heavy bleeding**, especially
  with **severe tummy pain** or feeling faint.
