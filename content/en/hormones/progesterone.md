## Glance
Progesterone is the hormone of the cycle's second half, preparing the womb lining after ovulation.

## Overview
Progesterone is one of the main hormones behind the menstrual cycle, and it takes the lead in the
second half — after an egg is released at ovulation.

Its main role is to help the lining of the uterus (womb) stay thick and ready, so that if a
fertilised egg arrives it can implant and a pregnancy can begin. In this way progesterone works as
a partner to estrogen, which did much of the build-up earlier in the cycle.

If no pregnancy begins, progesterone levels fall. That drop is what allows the lining to be shed
as your next period. The rise and fall of progesterone across the second half of the cycle is a
normal, expected rhythm.

## Deep
Progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum — the structure the ovary forms from the
follicle after it has released its egg. This is why progesterone rises specifically in the luteal
(post-ovulation) phase. When the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone and estrogen both fall,
signalling the start of a new cycle. Its natural shifts are part of why some people notice
premenstrual changes in the days before a period.
