## Glance
Feeling or being sick is very common in early pregnancy, and usually eases by around weeks 16 to 20.

## Overview
Feeling sick, or being sick, is very common in early pregnancy. Despite the name "morning sickness,"
it can happen at any time of the day or night. For most people it starts in the first few weeks and
settles down by around 16 to 20 weeks.

It's unpleasant, and for some it really affects day-to-day life — but ordinary morning sickness does
not put your baby at any increased risk. Eating small amounts often, and finding what helps you, can
make it more bearable; a midwife, GP or pharmacist can suggest safe options if you're struggling.

For a small number of people the sickness becomes severe (this is called hyperemesis gravidarum),
with a real risk of becoming dehydrated. That version needs medical help — see the signs below.

## When to see a doctor
Contact your midwife, GP, or NHS 111 if you:

- cannot keep any food or drink down for 24 hours
- have very dark urine, or do not pee for more than 8 hours
- feel very weak, dizzy, or faint when you stand up
- have tummy pain, a high temperature, or you're vomiting blood
- are losing weight

## Red flags
- Being unable to keep fluids down, with very dark or no urine, can mean you're getting dehydrated
  (severe pregnancy sickness) — **contact your midwife, GP or NHS 111 the same day**, as you may
  need treatment to stop it getting worse.
