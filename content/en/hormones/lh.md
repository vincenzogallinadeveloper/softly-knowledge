## Glance
LH is the brain hormone whose mid-cycle surge triggers the ovary to release an egg.

## Overview
LH — luteinizing hormone — is the messenger that sets off ovulation. Like FSH, it's made by a
small gland in the brain (the pituitary) and it tells the ovary when to release an egg.

For most of the first half of the cycle LH stays low. Then, around the middle of the cycle, it
rises sharply in what's called the LH surge. This surge is the signal that causes the ovary to
release its egg — ovulation — usually a day or so later.

The surge is a brief, well-timed signal rather than something you'd notice in how you feel. Because
it comes so closely before the egg is released, the timing of the LH rise is a reliable marker of
the most fertile part of the cycle.

## Deep
LH works together with FSH, the hormone that grows the follicles earlier in the cycle. Where FSH
leads the build-up, the LH surge is the turning point: levels begin climbing roughly a day and a
half before the egg is released and peak about half a day before it. After the surge, the cycle
moves into its second half. This tight timing is why LH is such a useful marker for anyone tracking
ovulation, and why it's central to how fertility treatments are planned.
