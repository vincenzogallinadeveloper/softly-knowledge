## Glance
Stress, big changes in weight, or lots of exercise can delay or stop a period — often just for a while.

## Overview
It's common for a period to arrive late, or to skip a month, and the reason often has more to do
with life than with anything being wrong. Stress is a frequent one — the same system that responds
to pressure can nudge the hormones that run your cycle.

Other everyday things can shift your periods too: a big change in weight (up or down), doing a lot
of exercise, or being run down. Hormonal changes — such as the approach of perimenopause, or
conditions like PCOS — can also be behind it, as can pregnancy and some contraception. Often the gap
between periods simply varies a little from time to time, and that's normal.

The reassuring part is that a one-off late or missed period usually isn't a cause for concern, and
things often settle as life does. What's worth watching is a pattern of change rather than a single
month.

## When to see a doctor
See a GP if:

- you've missed **three periods in a row**, or your periods have become **irregular**
- your periods **stop** and you're not sure why (and pregnancy is possible, it's worth a test first)
- missed periods come with other changes — like unexpected weight changes, tiredness, extra hair
  growth, or feeling low

These checks are just to understand what's behind the change — which is usually something
manageable.
