## Glance
The coil is a small device placed in the womb — copper (IUD) or hormonal (IUS) — that lasts years.

## Overview
The coil is a small device a clinician places inside the womb. There are two kinds, and it helps to
tell them apart:

- The **IUD**, or copper coil, contains no hormones. The copper changes conditions in the womb so
  that sperm and eggs can't survive well, and it also affects cervical mucus. Depending on the type,
  it works for around 5 to 10 years.
- The **IUS**, or hormonal coil, releases a progestogen instead of using copper. It thickens the
  mucus at the neck of the womb and thins the womb lining; for some people it can also make periods
  much lighter.

Both are "fit and forget" methods and both are over 99% effective, with little difference between
perfect and typical use. Neither protects against STIs.

## Deep
The coils are long-acting reversible methods: once fitted they keep working for years, and fertility
returns after they're removed. Fitting and removal are done by a trained clinician and take only a
few minutes, though some people find fitting uncomfortable. A common reason people choose between
the two is their effect on periods — the copper IUD can make periods heavier at first, while the
hormonal IUS often makes them lighter. Which suits you is a conversation with a clinician; this atom
just explains how each works.
