## Glance
Brown discharge is usually a little blood, often on the lighter days of a period. Brown spotting at other times is worth getting checked.

## Overview
Brown discharge means a small amount of brownish blood or fluid that you notice on your underwear
or on toilet paper. Many people see it at some point, and very often it's simply part of a period.

Period blood isn't always bright red. On your heaviest days it tends to be red, but on lighter days
it can look pink or brown instead. So a brownish tinge on the lighter days of your period is a
common, normal thing.

Brown can also show up at other times. Spotting means small spots of pink, red or brown blood, and
spotting between periods has several common causes. Hormonal contraception, such as the pill, is a
frequent one. Others include small growths in or around the womb (such as polyps or fibroids) and
infections such as chlamydia. In very early pregnancy, some people notice a little spotting around
the time their period would be due. This is known as implantation bleeding.

Because brown spotting outside your period can come from different causes, most of them minor, the
helpful step isn't to guess. It's to have it looked at, so you know what's behind it for you.

## When to see a doctor
- See a GP or a sexual health clinic if you have **brown spotting or bleeding between periods or
  after sex**, even if it's light. It's usually not serious, but it's always worth getting checked.
- If you're **pregnant, or might be**, contact your maternity unit, an early pregnancy unit or
  **NHS 111** about any spotting, including brown spotting, so the cause can be checked.
- See a GP if you notice **any brown or pink discharge or bleeding after your menopause**, even if
  it's only a small amount or has only happened once.
- Get advice from a GP, a sexual health clinic or **NHS 111** if your discharge **changes colour,
  smell or texture**, or comes with itching, soreness, pelvic pain or pain when you pee.

## Red flags
- Ask for an **urgent** GP appointment or call **NHS 111** if you have **recently missed a period
  and have unusual bleeding along with tummy or pelvic pain**. This needs to be checked quickly, as
  it can be a sign of an ectopic pregnancy.
- If you're pregnant, call **999** if you're bleeding and have **severe tummy pain, pain in your
  shoulder, feel faint, dizzy or sick, or the bleeding is heavy** (soaking a pad soon after putting
  it on).
