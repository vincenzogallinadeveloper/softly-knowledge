## Glance
There's no evidence the combined pill causes weight gain, but the contraceptive injection can. It depends on the method.

## Overview
Wondering whether contraception will make you put on weight is a very common question. The honest
answer is that it depends on the method.

For the combined pill, the evidence is reassuring: there's no evidence that taking it makes you gain
weight. The same is true of the contraceptive patch and the vaginal ring.

The contraceptive injection is different. Putting on weight is listed as one of its common side
effects, alongside changes to your periods.

For the progestogen-only pill (sometimes called the mini pill) and the contraceptive implant, some
people report gaining weight. But there isn't enough evidence to say whether the method itself is
the cause.

Weight is only one part of the picture. Each method has its own list of possible side effects and
risks, and choosing contraception is about finding what suits your body, your health and your
life. A pharmacist, GP, nurse or sexual health clinic can go through the options with you.

## When to see a doctor
- If side effects, including changes to your weight, are a problem for you, speak to a pharmacist,
  GP, nurse or sexual health clinic. With the combined pill, the patch or the ring, some side effects
  can happen when you first start. If they're still bothering you after about 3 months, a clinician
  may talk with you about a different pill or another type of contraception.
