## Glance
Group B strep is a common, usually harmless bacterium — the small risk is to the baby during birth, and it's manageable.

## Overview
Group B strep (GBS) is a very common bacterium that lots of people carry, usually in the vagina or
bottom, without knowing and without it causing any harm. It isn't a sexually transmitted infection,
and carrying it is normal.

In pregnancy it matters only because there's a **small chance** it can pass to the baby around the
time of birth and, rarely, make them unwell — this happens in roughly 1 in 1,750 pregnancies. It's
often found by chance, for example on a urine test or a vaginal swab done for another reason (the NHS
doesn't routinely screen everyone for it).

The reassuring part is that it's very manageable: if GBS is known about, having **antibiotics through
a drip during labour** greatly lowers the chance of the baby becoming ill.

## When to see a doctor
- If you're told you carry group B strep — now or in a past pregnancy — **let your midwife or
  maternity team know**, so antibiotics during labour can be planned. It's a simple step that makes a
  real difference.
