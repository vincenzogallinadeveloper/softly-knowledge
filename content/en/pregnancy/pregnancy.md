## Glance
Pregnancy usually lasts about 40 weeks, counted from the first day of your last period.

## Overview
Pregnancy is the time your body grows and carries a baby. It normally lasts about 40 weeks —
anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks is considered full and normal — and, perhaps surprisingly, it's counted
from the first day of your **last period**, not from conception. That's just the standard way dates
are worked out.

Your **due date** is an estimate, not a deadline: babies happily arrive a bit earlier or later. Early
in pregnancy a "dating scan" (usually around 12 weeks) can measure your baby and give a more accurate
date, which sometimes shifts the original estimate.

Pregnancy is often talked about in three stages, or **trimesters**, each with its own changes. All
the way through, you're looked after with regular antenatal check-ups from a midwife (and a doctor if
needed) — the care that helps keep you and your baby well.

## Deep
Because dates are counted from the last period, you're considered around four weeks pregnant by the
time a period is missed. The weeks-of-pregnancy count is how care is organised — scans, screening,
and check-ups are all offered at particular weeks. If your periods are irregular or you're unsure of
dates, the dating scan is what sets the timeline. This atom is the map of the whole journey; the
specific topics — signs of pregnancy, antenatal care, keeping well, and labour — each have their own.
