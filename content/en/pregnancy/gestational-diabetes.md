## Glance
Gestational diabetes is raised blood sugar during pregnancy — usually found by screening, and it often settles after birth.

## Overview
Gestational diabetes is high blood sugar (glucose) that develops during pregnancy, when the body
can't quite keep up with the extra demand for insulin. For most people it goes away again after the
baby is born.

It usually causes **no symptoms**, so it's picked up through a screening test offered in pregnancy
(a glucose tolerance test, often around 24 to 28 weeks), especially if you have things that raise the
chance of it — like a higher weight, a family history of diabetes, a previous large baby, or certain
ethnic backgrounds.

It's very manageable. Keeping blood sugar in a healthy range — through food, activity, and sometimes
medicine, all guided by your care team — lowers the chance of complications for you and your baby,
which is why it's worth staying on top of.

## When to see a doctor
- Take up the **screening test** if it's offered — since gestational diabetes usually has no
  symptoms, testing is how it's found.
- Don't wait for the test if you notice possible signs of high blood sugar — feeling very thirsty,
  needing to pee a lot, a dry mouth, or tiredness — mention them to your midwife or doctor.
