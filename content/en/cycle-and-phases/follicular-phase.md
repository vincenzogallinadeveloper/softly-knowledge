## Glance
The follicular phase is the first half of the cycle, when an egg ripens and the womb lining thickens.

## Overview
The follicular phase is the first part of the cycle. It begins on the first day of your period and
runs up to ovulation. Its name comes from the follicle — the small fluid-filled sac in the ovary
that holds and grows an egg during this time.

Through this phase the hormone estrogen rises. That rising estrogen does two main things: it helps
a follicle in the ovary mature, and it thickens the lining of the uterus (womb), getting the body
ready in case a pregnancy begins later in the cycle.

Because estrogen is climbing, some people notice more energy or changes in cervical mucus as the
fertile days approach. Everyone's experience is different, and none of it needs "managing" — it's
simply the first half of your cycle doing its work.

## Deep
The follicular phase can vary in length from cycle to cycle, which is part of why whole cycles
differ from person to person and month to month. It ends when rising estrogen helps trigger the
release of an egg (ovulation). Because its final days lead into ovulation, this is when the fertile
window opens — which is why the end of the follicular phase matters for anyone tracking fertility.
