## Glance
The safest approach is not to drink any alcohol in pregnancy or when trying to conceive.

## Overview
The NHS advice is clear and simple: the safest approach is **not to drink alcohol at all** if you're
pregnant or trying to conceive. Not drinking keeps any risk to your baby to a minimum.

The reason is that alcohol passes from your blood, through the placenta, to your baby — whose liver
isn't developed enough to process it. Drinking in pregnancy can lead to fetal alcohol spectrum
disorder (FASD), a range of lifelong difficulties with learning, behaviour, and development.

If you drank before you knew you were pregnant, try not to worry: the risk to the baby from small
amounts early on is likely to be low. The helpful thing is simply to avoid alcohol for the rest of
the pregnancy from now on.

## When to see a doctor
- If you're finding it hard to stop drinking, that's nothing to be ashamed of — tell your midwife or
  GP. They can offer support without judgement, and getting help is good for you and your baby.
