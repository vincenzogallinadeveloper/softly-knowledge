## Glance
Folic acid before and in early pregnancy helps protect the baby — plus a daily vitamin D.

## Overview
Two supplements really matter in pregnancy, and the NHS recommends both.

**Folic acid** is the big one. Taking a 400 microgram supplement every day — ideally from before you
conceive (about three months ahead) and until the end of the first trimester, at 12 weeks — helps
protect the baby against neural tube defects such as spina bifida. Because it works so early, it's
one of the few things worth starting *before* a pregnancy if you're trying.

**Vitamin D** is the other: a daily 10 microgram supplement is recommended right through pregnancy
and while breastfeeding, to support the baby's bones and development.

Beyond those two, a healthy varied diet covers most needs — you don't need lots of extra supplements.
One to steer clear of is **vitamin A** (retinol): don't take supplements containing it, and avoid
liver, as too much vitamin A can harm the baby.

## When to see a doctor
- A **GP, midwife, or pharmacist** can help you choose the right supplements, and some people are
  advised to take a **higher dose of folic acid** (for example with diabetes, a higher BMI, certain
  medicines, or a previous pregnancy affected by a neural tube defect) — so it's worth asking,
  especially before or in early pregnancy.
