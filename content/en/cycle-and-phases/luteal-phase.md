## Glance
The luteal phase is the second half of the cycle, after ovulation, when progesterone rises.

## Overview
The luteal phase is the part of the cycle that follows ovulation and runs until your next period
begins. It's named after the corpus luteum — the small structure the ovary forms from the empty
follicle once the egg has been released.

In this phase the hormone progesterone rises. Its job is to keep the lining of the uterus (womb)
thick and settled, so that if a fertilised egg arrives it has somewhere to implant. Estrogen stays
part of the picture too, working alongside progesterone.

If no pregnancy begins, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone and estrogen fall, and the
lining is no longer maintained — which brings on your next period and starts a new cycle. This is
also the stretch when some people notice premenstrual changes in mood, sleep or the body.

## Deep
The luteal phase tends to be a little steadier in length than the first half of the cycle, often
running somewhere in the region of about two weeks, though there's normal variation from person to
person. Because its end is tied to the fall in progesterone, the arrival of a period is really the
visible sign that hormone levels have dropped. Tracking how you feel across the luteal phase can
help you tell apart what's a familiar pattern for you from what's new or worth a conversation with
a clinician.
