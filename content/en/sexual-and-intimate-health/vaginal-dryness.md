## Glance
Vaginal dryness is common, often linked to hormones, and can cause soreness or discomfort during sex.

## Overview
Vaginal dryness is a common experience that many people have at some point. It happens when the
usual moisture that keeps the vagina comfortable is reduced, and it can feel like soreness,
itching, or discomfort — including pain or discomfort during sex.

It's often linked to hormones, especially a fall in estrogen. That's why it's common around
menopause and perimenopause, but it can also happen during breastfeeding, with some medicines (such
as certain contraceptives or antidepressants), or when there isn't enough arousal before sex. It's
nothing to feel embarrassed about — it's a physical thing, not a reflection of you or your
relationship.

Knowing it's common, and usually very treatable, is a good starting point. There are simple options
that can help, and a GP or pharmacist can point you to what suits you.

## When to see a doctor
See a GP if:

- vaginal dryness is bothering you or doesn't settle after a few weeks
- it's affecting your daily life or your sex life
- you also have unusual discharge, or any bleeding after sex, between periods, or after menopause

There's no need to just put up with it — help is available, and a clinician can look at what's
behind it and what might ease it.
