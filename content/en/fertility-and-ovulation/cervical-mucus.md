## Glance
Cervical mucus is the natural discharge that changes through the cycle and marks your fertile days.

## Overview
Cervical mucus is the fluid the cervix makes, which you may notice as discharge. It's normal, and
its look and feel change in a fairly predictable pattern across the cycle — which is why it's one
of the signs used to spot the fertile days.

Just after a period there are often a few "dry" days with little mucus. As an egg begins to
mature, mucus increases and tends to be white or yellow, cloudy and sticky. In the days right
before ovulation it usually turns clear, wet and slippery, stretching between the fingers like raw
egg white — this is the most fertile time. After ovulation it typically becomes sticky and cloudy
again, or dries up.

Everyone's pattern is a little different, and it can take a few cycles of paying attention to learn
your own. Some things — such as hormonal contraception, breastfeeding, or an infection — can change
the mucus, so it isn't a reliable fertility sign for everyone.

## Deep
Reading cervical mucus is one of the fertility-awareness signs people track, alongside resting body
temperature and cycle length; learning them usually takes two to three cycles. Unlike a temperature
rise, which confirms ovulation only after it has happened, the shift to clear, slippery, egg-white
mucus tends to appear *before* ovulation, which is why it's used to flag the fertile window as it
opens rather than after it closes.
