## Glance
Condoms are a barrier method — and the only kind of contraception that also protects against STIs.

## Overview
A condom is a thin barrier — usually latex, or a plastic alternative for those with a latex allergy
— worn on the penis during sex. It works simply: it stops sperm from entering the vagina.

What makes condoms special among contraceptives is that they're **the only method that also protects
against sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV.** That's why they're often used
alongside another method — one for pregnancy, both together for infection protection.

Used correctly every time, condoms are about 98% effective at preventing pregnancy. With typical
use — where they're sometimes used incorrectly or not every time — that drops to around 82%. Using
one properly and consistently is what makes the difference.

## Deep
Because condoms are used in the moment rather than in advance, their real-world effectiveness
depends heavily on consistent, correct use. Internal condoms (worn inside the vagina) are another
barrier option. Unlike most methods, condoms need no prescription and are widely available, and
their dual role — pregnancy and STI protection — is why they come up so often in conversations about
safer sex.
