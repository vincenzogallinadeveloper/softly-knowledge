## Glance
Cervical screening is a routine check of the health of your cervix that helps prevent cervical cancer.

## Overview
Cervical screening is a test that checks the health of the cervix — the opening to the womb at the
top of the vagina. It isn't a test for cancer. Instead, it's a way to spot changes early and help
prevent cervical cancer from developing in the first place.

Everyone with a cervix is invited from the age of 25 up to 64. You're sent an invitation when
you're due, at regular intervals, so you don't need to remember to book it out of the blue —
though it's always your choice whether to go.

The test itself is quick and done by a nurse. Many people feel a bit nervous about it, which is
completely understandable; knowing what it's for — a routine, preventive check — can make it feel
more manageable.

## When to see a doctor
- Screening is for when you **don't** have symptoms. If you notice something that worries you
  between screenings — such as unusual bleeding (between periods, after sex, or after menopause) —
  see a GP rather than waiting for your next invitation.
- If you've been invited and you're unsure or anxious about going, it's worth talking to your GP or
  practice nurse, who can explain what happens and answer your questions.
