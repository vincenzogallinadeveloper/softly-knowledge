## Glance
There are several ways to ease labour pain — there's no right or wrong choice, and you can stay flexible.

## Overview
Labour is different for everyone, and there are several ways to cope with the pain — from simple
comfort measures to medical options. Knowing what's available ahead of time can help you feel more
prepared.

Options range from **staying active and upright**, breathing and relaxation, massage, and being in
**water** (a bath or birth pool), through to **a TENS machine**, **gas and air** (Entonox, which you
breathe yourself), **injections** like pethidine, and an **epidural** (given by an anaesthetist,
which numbs the pain of labour more fully).

There's no "best" choice — it depends on you, your labour, and how you feel on the day. It's worth
noting your preferences in a **birth plan**, while keeping an open mind: many people find they want
more, or less, pain relief than they expected, and that's completely fine.

## When to see a doctor
- Your **midwife** (and, for an epidural, an anaesthetist) will talk you through the options and help
  you decide what suits you — during pregnancy and again during labour itself. There's no need to
  decide everything in advance.
