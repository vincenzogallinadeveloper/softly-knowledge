## Glance
Her body moves through a monthly cycle with four phases — knowing the basics helps you support her without guessing.

## Overview
The menstrual cycle is a roughly monthly rhythm, on average about 28 days but often anywhere from 21
to 35, and normal for her is whatever is regular for *her*. It runs in four phases: the **menstrual**
phase (her period), the **follicular** phase, **ovulation** around the middle, and the **luteal**
phase before the next period.

Energy, mood, sleep and desire can shift across these phases because hormones rise and fall — that's
biology, not a character flaw and not something to tease her about. Some people feel a real dip in the
days before their period; others barely notice. There's no single "right" way to feel.

You don't need to become an expert. What helps most is simple: know roughly where she is in her cycle
(this app can show you, with her permission), expect that some days are harder than others, and ask
her what *she* finds helpful rather than assuming.

## Deep
A few things worth knowing, so myths don't fill the gap:

- **"On her period" isn't an insult or an explanation for disagreeing with her.** Using her cycle to
  dismiss her feelings ("you're just hormonal") is disrespectful, and it's usually wrong. Her reasons
  are still her reasons.
- **The cycle affects more than periods.** Cramps, headaches, tender breasts, low energy, or lower
  mood can all be part of it. Practical help beats advice.
- **Tracking is hers.** If she shares her cycle with you, treat it as a window into how she might be
  feeling — an invitation to be kind, never a tool to monitor or control her.
- **When in doubt, ask.** "How are you feeling today? Anything I can do?" is worth more than any chart.
