## Glance
Periods can bring real pain and tiredness — small, practical care makes a big difference.

## Overview
A period isn't just a few days of bleeding; for many it comes with cramps, back pain, headaches,
tiredness and low energy. Period pain is common and can be genuinely draining — it isn't an
exaggeration. Treat it the way you'd treat any partner who's unwell: with practical kindness.

Normalise it, too. Periods aren't gross, embarrassing or a taboo. Being comfortable buying pads or
tampons, keeping some at home, or handing her a hot water bottle without fuss tells her she's safe and
supported.

## Deep
Simple things that help:

- **Warmth and rest.** A hot water bottle or heat pad on the lower belly eases cramps; offer a quiet
  evening in.
- **Take things off her plate.** Cook, tidy, run the errand, drive her — practical help lands harder
  than words.
- **Keep supplies in.** Know what she uses and keep a stock, so a heavy day is never a crisis.
- **Painkillers and comfort food** if she wants them; her choice.
- **Don't pressure for sex or energy she doesn't have.** Closeness can be a cuddle on the sofa.

## When to see a doctor
Encourage her to see a doctor (and offer to go with her) if period pain is **severe, getting worse,
or stopping her from daily life**, if periods are **very heavy** (soaking through protection hourly,
large clots), or if pain is new and unusual — these can point to conditions like endometriosis that
deserve proper care.
